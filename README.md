# Crossblades

Skill-based directional melee combat for Minecraft, inspired by Chivalry and For Honor.
Flick your mouse to choose where your swing comes from, block in the right direction at the right
moment, and parry by attacking into your opponent's swing.

**Minecraft 26.1.2 · Fabric and NeoForge**

## Requirements

- Minecraft **26.1.2**
- **Fabric** (with Fabric API) or **NeoForge**
- **[Player Animation Library](https://modrinth.com/mod/player-animation-library)** for 26.1.x

Everyone on a server needs the mod. On NeoForge, Player Animation Library is only required on clients.

## How to fight

Hold a sword or axe. Combat stance is on by default (toggle it with **R** to chop wood or mine
normally with an axe).

| Input | What it does |
|---|---|
| Flick mouse **up** | Load an **overhead** (slow, strong) and set your guard **up** |
| Flick mouse **left** / **right** | Load a **slash** from that side (wide, can hit 3) and set your guard to that side |
| Flick mouse **down** | Load a **poke** (fast, long reach, weaker). Your guard stays where it was |
| **Left click** | Swing the loaded attack |
| Hold **right click** | Guard in your current guard direction |

The small arrow next to the crosshair shows your loaded attack (white = ready, orange = winding up,
grey = recovering). A blue bar shows your guard while you block. Nothing on screen shows your
opponent's attack: read their wind-up animation.

After a flick you usually re-aim at your target. Moving back the opposite way right after a flick is
ignored, so flick-right-then-re-center keeps "right".

### Blocking

Guard the side the weapon is coming from **on your screen**: an enemy swinging from their right
comes at your left, so guard left. Overheads need an up guard. Pokes are stopped by a left or right guard.

The later you raise your guard before the hit, the less damage you take:

| Guard raised before the hit | Damage taken |
|---|---|
| under 100 ms | 0% (**perfect block**) |
| 100–200 ms | 20% |
| 200–300 ms | 40% |
| 300–400 ms | 60% |
| 400 ms or more | 80% |

Changing guard direction restarts the timer. You can't block hits from behind or the side.

### Parrying

Instead of blocking, **attack into their swing**: start an attack toward the side their weapon is
coming from (the side you'd guard) within 100 ms of their hit. The parried attacker is staggered
(can't attack or block) and your attack becomes a fast riposte. Pick the wrong side and you take the
full hit.

### Trading and interrupts

A clean hit on someone who is winding up cancels their attack. If two swings land on the same
tick, both hit.

## Settings

Everything is in `config/crossblades.json` (created on first launch). On a server, the server's
file decides the combat rules; the `client` section only affects your own game.

- Wind-up, recovery, damage, reach and swing width for `overhead`, `slash` and `poke`
- `blockTiers`, `blockDamageAfterLastTier`: the blocking table above
- `parryWindowMs`, `parryStaggerMs`, `riposteWindupMs`
- `hitsInterruptWindups`, `hitFlinchMs`, `blockConeDegrees`
- `disableVanillaWeaponAttacks`: stops normal click-spam attacks with weapons
- `client.flickThresholdDegrees`, `client.flickWindowMs`: how hard you need to flick
- `client.firstPersonAnimations`, `client.showDirectionArrow`, `client.showFeedbackText`

Restart the game after changing it.

Which items count as weapons is the item tag `crossblades:melee_weapons` (swords and axes by
default), which you can change with a datapack.

## Building

```
./gradlew build
```

Jars end up in `fabric/build/libs` and `neoforge/build/libs`.

On every push, GitHub Actions:

1. runs the plain-Java tests for the combat rules (`tools/run-core-tests.sh`),
2. builds both jars,
3. starts the real game headless on Fabric and NeoForge and runs an in-game self-test that plays
   the combat loop with real inputs (flicks, clicks, holding block) against a husk and a dummy
   attacker, checking damage, block tiers, wrong-side hits and parries, and screenshots every pose,
4. starts a dedicated server on each loader to make sure it loads.

The latest logs and screenshots are kept on the `ci-logs*` branches.

The animations are generated by `tools/gen_animations.py` (Blockbench/GeckoLib format).
